wall comment treatments

The comment wall (กำแพงความในใจ) scatters approved messages across a field and drifts them gently upward. The current bubble is a hard-bordered rectangle with a bright Nixie glow — too loud, and it reads as a UI box rather than a message floating on a wall. These three treatments all drop the box and the glow and lean subtle: borderless or edge-feathered surfaces, ambient depth instead of emission, and quieter accents. Each preview is live — the atoms drift on the same compositor-friendly translateY bob the real wall uses.

Option A — Recommended

Transmission lines

most subtle · most on-theme · cheapest to build

No surface at all — each message is anchored only by a thin amber hairline on its left edge, like an intercepted lab transmission. A muted mono header (> @rintarou) tags the author; the body sits in the Thai sans. Zero box, zero glow, and it reads as "messages on the wall" rather than "cards in a grid." Scales cleanly to any length and is the lightest to render.

Option B

Feathered slips

keeps a surface · softest / most atmospheric

For when the messages still want a little body behind them. Each slip has a faint translucent fill, but its rectangle is masked away at the edges with a radial fade — so it dissolves into the wall instead of sitting in a hard frame. Depth comes from a soft downward ambient shadow, not emission. A short amber tick (not a glow) marks the author line. Cozy and tactile while still quiet.

Option C

Quote drift

most editorial · zero chrome

The purest take: no surface, no border, no rule — just the words, each opened by a large ghosted quotation mark in barely-there amber. It treats the wall as a page of floating epigraphs. Most elegant and the least "UI," though long messages lean on whitespace alone to separate, so it wants a bit more spacing between drifting atoms than A or B.

Recommendation: Option A (transmission lines) — it satisfies every ask in one move (no box, no glow, maximally subtle), reads most naturally as the Future Gadget Lab intercepting messages, and is the smallest diff: drop the border + box-shadow on comment-bubble.tsx, add a border-left and a mono header. Option B is the pick if you want to keep a faint surface for legibility over busy background decor; Option C if you want the wall to feel like an editorial page of quotes.
